# Business story


Why I started the business: As a busy high-school student, I needed a way to earn money without regularly working until 10:00 or 11:30 p.m. on weeknights. That schedule left me tired and stressed and gave me little time for school or personal responsibilities. I turned my interest in cleaning my own car into a business, researched professional detailing chemicals and techniques, and tested what I learned on my own vehicle before serving customers. I also wanted to learn business management, strengthen my confidence, and improve my communication skills. Detailing lets me use a detail-oriented skill set while working fewer, more flexible hours.
Operating philosophy: My goal has always been to work fewer hours while maximizing the operating contribution produced within the hours I choose to work. At my retail job, I was overworked, barely sleeping, and had little time for school or other responsibilities. Running my own detailing business gives me control over my schedule. I focus on keeping my effective hourly rate as high as possible through pricing, scheduling, efficient service delivery, and cost control. I measure growth by revenue and by how efficiently my business uses limited working hours.


What problem I solve: Many vehicle owners lack the time, equipment, or desire to complete a thorough detail themselves. I provide a convenient mobile service at the customer’s location, helping restore the vehicle’s cleanliness and appearance without requiring the customer to do the work. The site should describe the service and its value without making unverified claims about competitors or promising that every vehicle will be restored to brand-new condition.
Important business milestones: I started the company in January 2026 and had broken even on my initial expenses by the end of February. I began booking appointments weeks in advance in March. My January and February appointments are real completed business transactions that were added after the original booking export.
What my business demonstrates: Building and operating my business has taught me entrepreneurship, chemical safety, pricing, customer retention, scheduling, communication, inventory management, and process improvement. My business contributes to my independence while giving me greater control over my schedule. The site should present those lessons through my business data, operating decisions, and clear explanations of how I run the company.

# Required pages and features


Overview dashboard: Total booked revenue, completed revenue, scheduled revenue, completed and planned bookings, average booking value, repeat-booking rate, labor hours, and growth over time.


Booking analytics: Monthly bookings, revenue trends, vehicle mix, service mix, Hydroseal upgrades, completed versus planned bookings, and repeat customers.


Bookings calendar: Public calendar using aliases only. No real customer names, phone numbers, email addresses, street addresses, or private calendar descriptions.


Inventory: Item list, quantities, status, and pricing basis. Clearly distinguish recorded prices from current replacement-price research.


Expenses and funding: The recorded cash business expense is $22 for business cards. Compensation and gift cards I earned through market research for technology companies paid for my gas and approximately $400 of inventory, and I directed those resources toward business expenses. This is why my direct cash overhead remained low. Gas amounts are not tracked in the booking dataset, so they are excluded from calculated operating-contribution figures. The inventory is still disclosed as a business resource even though it was not a direct cash outlay.


Business story: Explain why I started my business, the problem I solve, my major milestones, my operating philosophy, and the lessons I learned while building and running it.


Project methodology: Every booking record represents a real completed business transaction or a real planned future booking commitment. Customer aliases conceal identities; no private customer information is included.


# Data privacy


All booking activity and future commitments are real. Customer aliases conceal identities. Completed bookings drive earned-revenue and realized-performance totals; planned bookings appear in the schedule and booked-revenue forecast.


Financial reporting definition: Cash-basis operating contribution equals revenue minus recorded cash business expenses. It is not accounting net profit because gas and per-booking material consumption are not fully quantified. State that market-research compensation and gift cards paid for gas and approximately $400 of inventory, keeping direct cash overhead low, while still showing that inventory as a business resource.
